# Wave 1 route: a geographically tight starting zone

**This is a proposal to verify on the ground, not a survey.** I don't have
access to a parcel/GIS tool from this session, so the street names and
boundaries below are grounded in real, current sources on Westfield's
neighborhoods (linked at the bottom), but the household count is an
estimate from typical block lengths, not a counted list of addresses. Walk
the boundary (or pull it up on the town's tax map / GIS viewer) before
ordering flyers, and adjust the edge to land on your target count.

## Why concentrate instead of spreading out

Route density is one of this business's core economics: pickup and return
only work as a business if one person can walk or drive a tight loop in a
morning, not drive across town for each stop. A 100–250 home pilot spread
across Union County tells you nothing about whether the _route_ is viable —
only a tight zone does. `docs/experiment-plan.md`'s phase 1 goal is
explicitly to validate demand **and** the operating model together.

## Recommended zone: Dudley Park / Kimball Avenue, and its immediate blocks

**Core:** Kimball Avenue between Lawrence Avenue and Elm Street — this is
Westfield's Kimball Avenue Historic District, the core of what's locally
called Dudley Park. It's two to three short blocks, entirely single-family
detached homes, immediately north of downtown and a short walk from the
train station and Mindowaskin Park.

**Why this zone specifically:**

- **Walkable and compact.** The whole core is under a quarter mile end to
  end — a single loop, not a driving route. That's the actual thing phase 1
  needs to prove out.
- **Single-family, not multi-unit.** Every home is a plausible customer
  with their own kitchen; no apartment buildings or shared entries to
  complicate pickup logistics for a first run.
- **Close to downtown.** Anyone who's shopped or eaten downtown recently
  has plausibly seen (or will see) other Westfield Knife Care marketing —
  a poster in a downtown shop window, a mention on Nextdoor — so the flyer
  isn't the household's only touchpoint. That's a real, if soft, advantage
  worth capturing in the source tag data (a Dudley Park order converting
  might be picking up some ambient downtown awareness that a flyer-only
  neighborhood wouldn't have).

**If the core doesn't reach your target count**, extend outward in this
order — each ring is still an easy extension of the same walking loop, all
verified real streets in the same immediate area:

1. The rest of Kimball Avenue beyond Elm/Lawrence, plus the Lawrence Avenue
   and Elm Street cross-block frontages themselves.
2. Toward downtown: the Boulevard Historic District (the Boulevard, Cottage
   Place, Park Street) — similar character, a few blocks south.
3. Toward the park: the near edge of The Gardens (Mountain Avenue and
   Highland Avenue, the earliest-developed blocks, within the "commuter-walkable"
   ring the town itself describes as a 12–18 minute walk to downtown).

## Sizing it

Older Westfield blocks like these typically run 60–75ft lot frontages, so a
normal block face (both sides, a few hundred feet) holds roughly 20–30
houses. The Kimball Avenue core alone (a few short blocks) plausibly lands
somewhere in the 60–120 household range; adding one ring (Lawrence/Elm
cross-frontages, or the near Boulevard blocks) plausibly gets you into the
150–250 range this experiment calls for. Confirm the real number by
walking it with a clipboard, or by pulling the parcel count from the town's
online tax map before you commit to a print quantity — see
`marketing/flyers/README.md` for print-run guidance once you have a count.

## Splitting A and B within the zone

Don't hand out all of flyer A on one street and all of flyer B on the next
— that confounds price with micro-geography (corner lot vs. mid-block,
morning-sun vs. shade side, whatever). Alternate by house within each block
face (odd house numbers get A, even get B, or literally alternate door to
door) so both arms see the same streets, the same block positions, and the
same walk. Log which arm went to which address as you go (a spreadsheet or
even a photo of a marked-up printout of the block is enough) — you'll want
it to sanity-check the funnel against actual delivered volume, and to spot
if either arm ended up skewed toward, say, corner houses or a particular
sub-block for some accidental reason.

## A caveat worth knowing before you read the results

Dudley Park and its immediate neighbors are among Westfield's more
established, higher-value residential blocks (Kimball Avenue is the town's
only _officially_ designated historic district). That's a feature for a
first test — established homeowners with fully-equipped kitchens are a
plausible early market for a $39–49 local service — but it does mean Wave
1's conversion rate and price sensitivity may not generalize directly to
every Westfield neighborhood. If Wave 1 validates demand, a natural Wave 2
comparison is a similarly-sized, similarly-walkable zone with a different
housing profile (a denser side of town, or one further from downtown) to
see whether the result holds.

## Legal note on delivery

Federal law reserves the mailbox itself for USPS; a door or storm-door
hanger avoids that question. Westfield's own rules on unsolicited door-to-door
material (canvassing/soliciting ordinances, if any apply to a simple
leave-behind rather than a knock-and-talk) are worth a quick check with the
town clerk's office before the first walk — a two-person pilot can't afford
a code complaint on day one.
